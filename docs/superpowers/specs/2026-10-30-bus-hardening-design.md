# Bus hardening design: total subscription bound, write deadline, test-support extraction

**Status:** Approved 2026-10-30 (self-approved under Matt's autonomous-approve mode; flagged in the PR body). Amended after approval, also self-approved and flagged: AC21, AC22 and AC23 and the reversal of Architect's call 10 (the pre-auth refusal send is now bounded) came from stage 6 panel rounds 1 and 2.
**Research inputs:** none new. This is the follow-up to PR #137 (web `/bus` push): it closes the Minors that the security and quality panel accepted in the Review log of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-30-web-bus-push-plan.md`. Reads `crates/aitm-mcp/src/{server,transport}.rs`, `crates/aitm/src/web/bridge.rs`, `crates/aitm/tests/web_bus_{concurrency,roles,push}.rs`, `crates/aitm/tests/support/`, `crates/aitm-mcp/tests/mcp.rs`.

## Intent

#137 let a `/bus` connection subscribe and receive pushes. Its panel accepted three follow-ups: (1) the 32-per-connection subscription cap is bypassable by opening many connections, and fan-out de-dups connections with a `Vec`, so each event costs O(subscriptions x connections) under the registry lock; (2) a subscribed peer that stops reading holds its task, its subscriptions and, for an operator, `any_role(Operator)` until the kernel fails the write, because no write is bounded; (3) the `/bus` test harness is copied into three files and the push-server builder three times in `mcp.rs`. This change does those three things. It also bounds one send they do not name, the bad-token refusal before authentication, because that send can block (the first draft of this spec said it could not; a Pong flood can fill the socket). See D3 and call 10.

## Goals

- The registry holds at most 4096 subscriptions across all connections. At that limit another subscribe is refused with its own tool error; unsubscribing or ending a connection frees slots.
- Fan-out (flow events, state pushes, the per-tool-call audit line, operator-only lines) costs O(subscriptions) under the lock and delivers exactly what it delivers today: once per matching connection.
- A peer that stops reading is disconnected within the write deadline after its socket buffers fill. Its subscriptions are removed and `any_role(Operator)` stops being pinned. This holds on the web bridge (both post-authentication send sites) and on the line transport (TCP and stdio).
- A peer that is slow but still reading is not disconnected.
- Before authentication, a peer that stops reading cannot hold a connection past a fixed bound either: the bad-token refusal send on the web bridge is bounded by the 5 s close-drain budget and the connection is dropped on expiry. The worst case before authentication is stated in D3.
- The `/bus` harness is defined once, `mcp.rs` builds push servers through one helper, no test is removed or weakened, and the test counts are unchanged (26 in the three `aitm` files, 73 in `mcp.rs` before new tests; 76 in `mcp.rs` at head, with the three added since).

## Non-goals

- No change to the audit-stream fan-out scope: `notifications/aitm/agent`, `push_agent` and `agent.activity` role scoping stay as they are (a separate decision for Matt).
- No web listener connection cap. A named follow-up (the security reviewer's recommended next hardening item); see the known residual in the Threat/Failure model.
- No bound on the websocket library's write buffer and no ping budget. The Ping/Pong write-buffer growth in the known residuals is a named follow-up that needs a spec and a panel of its own.
- `ConnGuard` stays in `aitm`; it is not moved into `aitm-mcp`.
- No config-file, CLI or env surface for the new limits. `MAX_SUBS_TOTAL` is a constant; the write deadline has a default and a builder method, nothing more.
- No change to the streamable-HTTP transport, role/policy/scope gating, or the wire protocol apart from the new refusal text and connections ending at the deadline.
- No idle-connection liveness (ping/pong or TCP keepalive). The deadline only fires while the server is writing.
- No change to the pre-auth authentication logic or to `close_within` in `bridge.rs`. The one pre-auth change is that the bad-token refusal's reply send is bounded (D3, amended after panel round 1).

## Anti-goals

- A deadline that fires on an idle connection, or on a reader that is slow but making progress.
- A deadline that ends the connection but leaves its subscriptions, or leaves the line transport's read loop parked on a live socket.
- A deadline or fan-out test that is a timing benchmark and flakes on a loaded machine.
- A "table full" refusal that reads like the per-connection one, or one surfaced differently from it.
- An extraction that weakens a test: a test body edited, a helper made more lenient than the strictest copy it replaces, a test dropped or renamed.

## Constraints

- No new dependency. `tokio::net::TcpSocket` and `tokio::time` are already available to `aitm`. `aitm-mcp`'s library has never used `tokio::time`, so its regular `tokio` features gain `time`.
- Memory and CPU: one `HashSet<u64>` per fan-out call (no allocation until the first insert); one timer per send or write. Both are negligible next to the send.
- Sandbox: unit tests and the `tokio::io::duplex` tests run in the Bash sandbox. Tests that bind loopback (the bridge tests, the TCP test) run in the Terminal panel; the Coder compiles them and says which it could not run.
- Gate: `cargo fmt --check && cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings && cargo test`.

## Design

**D1. Total registry bound.** `const MAX_SUBS_TOTAL: usize = 4096` in `server.rs` beside `MAX_SUBS_PER_CONN`, with a const assertion that it is at least the per-connection cap. `Subscriptions::add` checks, under the one registry lock and in this order: the sink is closed (`Closed`); the connection already holds `MAX_SUBS_PER_CONN` (`TooMany`); the registry already holds `MAX_SUBS_TOTAL` (new `SubscribeRefused::TableFull`). **(Architect's call)** Precedence is closed, then per-connection, then table: the per-connection cause is the one the client controls and can fix, and it keeps today's texts and tests unchanged whatever the global load. `McpServer::subscribe` maps `TableFull` to `tool_error`, in the same shape as the `TooMany` arm: text `flow.subscribe refused: the server's subscription table is full (max 4096 across all connections); try again later`, `isError: true`, no `sub_id`, and no `since` replay (the refusal returns before it). It is audited like every other refused subscribe by the existing path; the comment in `call_tool` that lists the refusal reasons gains it. 4096 is 128 connections at the per-connection cap, far above real use (a TUI, a web UI, a Mac app, a few agents); registry memory at the cap is under 10 MiB (each entry holds at most two 1 KiB filter strings). The cap is per `McpServer`: `aitm web` runs two servers (primary and web), so the process-wide figure is 2 x 4096.

**D2. Fan-out de-dup.** `fanout_flow`, `fanout_state` and `fanout_role` replace `seen: Vec<u64>` and `contains` with a `HashSet<u64>` and `insert`. The trap: in `fanout_flow` (filter) and `fanout_role` (role) the connection is inserted only after the subscription passes its test. Inserting first would let a non-matching subscription mask a later matching one on the same connection, a silent missed delivery. Why it matters at the cap: worst case is 4096 connections with one subscription each, which with a `Vec` is about 8.4 million comparisons per event under the lock, and `push_agent` does the same for every finished tool call; a `HashSet` makes it 4096 inserts. Tested behaviourally plus one mechanical check (AC5, AC6); there is no timing assertion.

**D3. Write deadline.**
- **Value (Architect's call):** `pub const DEFAULT_WRITE_TIMEOUT: Duration = 30 s` in `server.rs`, re-exported next to `DEFAULT_PUSH_CAPACITY`. It is 3x the 10 s handshake budget and 6x the 5 s close drain; it lets one 16 MiB frame finish at about 0.53 MiB/s (4.3 Mbit/s), far below any loopback or LAN reader; and it is short enough that a dead peer is gone in well under a minute after its buffers fill.
- **Semantics:** the budget is per send (one websocket frame) or per `write_all`/`flush` call. The clock starts when the send starts and stops when the socket has accepted the frame, not when the peer reads it.
- **Seam (Architect's call):** `McpServer::with_write_timeout(Duration)` (stored clamped to at least 1 ms) and `McpServer::write_timeout() -> Duration`, the same shape as `with_push_capacity`/`push_capacity`. The bridge already reads `state.mcp.push_capacity()` and `serve_lines` already reads `server.push_capacity()`, so one setting serves both transports, and a test lowers it without touching `WebState` (several struct literals, `main.rs` among them) and without an env var. `serve(self)` destructures and rebuilds `McpServer` field by field, so the new field is added in both places.
- **Bridge:** a private `send_within` wraps `ws.send(..)` in `tokio::time::timeout(write_timeout, ..)` and is used at both post-auth send sites, the frame-done reply arm and the drain arm. Expiry returns an error from `relay`; the existing `ConnGuard` then closes the sink and drops the subscriptions. No Close handshake is attempted: a peer that is not reading cannot answer it, and `close_within` would spend up to 5 s more. Frames still in flight are detached, as on any other exit. The error reaches `run`'s existing `debug!` line; no new log, and nothing token-bearing.
- **Pre-auth refusal (amended after panel round 1):** `HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` bounds only the wait for the token, not the `UNAUTHORIZED_REPLY` send that answers a bad one, and that send can block. A peer that sends Pings and never reads fills the send path with tungstenite's automatic Pongs, then sends a bad token; an unbounded send would then pend for as long as the peer stays silent, pinning the task, the fd and the upgraded connection. The reply is sent through `send_within` with the 5 s close-drain budget; on expiry the connection is dropped and no Close is attempted. The Close that follows a successful reply keeps its own bound in `close_within`. The weakness predates this change (it is in #137's bridge).
- **Worst-case hold before authentication** (so nothing reads as unbounded): a bad token takes at most 10 s waiting for the token (`HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`, an absolute deadline that Pings do not extend) + 5 s for the reply send + 5 s for the Close in `close_within` = 20 s. The timeout path (no token arrives) and the stalled-reply path (the reply send expires, no Close) take 15 s. Before the upgrade the request head may take up to 15 s (`HEADER_READ_TIMEOUT` in `web/http.rs`). So an unauthenticated connection holds its task and descriptor for about 35 s at most; no step is unbounded. Memory is a separate matter (the Ping/Pong residual).
- **Line transport:** `write_loop` wraps each `write_all` and the `flush` in the same deadline and returns an error on expiry; `serve_lines` passes it `server.write_timeout()`. **(Architect's call)** This alone is not enough. `serve_lines` parks its read loop in `read_line` while a live peer is not reading, and it only drops the connection's subscriptions after that loop ends, so a writer that dies on the deadline would leave the connection and its subscriptions in place (a stopped-but-open TCP client never sends EOF). So when the writer ends in an error before the read loop does, `serve_lines` abandons the read loop, closes the sink, drops the connection's subscriptions and returns that error. Any writer error does this, not only the deadline: a failed write ends the connection too. A writer task that panics counts as a failure and ends the connection the same way (AC21). A writer that ends cleanly keeps today's behaviour: the read loop ends the connection.
  - **Teardown order, on every exit:** close the sink, then drop the connection. This is the order `ConnGuard` uses on the bridge, and the one `Subscriptions::add` relies on: a `flow.subscribe` still in flight is either refused (the sink is closed) or swept (it registered before the close, so the drop removes it). The reverse order leaves a window for a subscription to outlive its connection.
  - **The order is not pinned by a deterministic test on the line transport.** Swapping the two lines in `serve_lines` keeps every lib test green: only a writer panic reaches the wrong order, and a `flow.subscribe` racing the teardown is hard to stage. It is guarded by the comments, by the registry's closed-sink refusal in `Subscriptions::add`, and, on the bridge only, by the guard test `a_frame_still_running_at_disconnect_cannot_resubscribe`, which exercises a subscribe after the teardown rather than the window between its two steps.
  - **What proves what:** the duplex tests prove the abandonment and the cleanup: AC14 for a reader that stops while the read loop is parked in `read_line`, `a_client_that_floods_requests_and_never_reads_replies_is_disconnected` for one that keeps sending and is held back by reply backpressure, and AC21 for a panic. The real-TCP test (AC16) proves less: that the deadline reaches a real socket and the server stops writing and half-closes it. It cannot prove cleanup, because dropping the write half sends a FIN whether or not the writer's error is acted on; a mutation that ignored the error left it green.
- **stdio (Architect's call, flagged):** the stdio connection shares `serve_lines`, so a parent that stops reading our stdout for 30 s while we write ends the stdio connection. Its blast radius is the whole process. In `main` the MCP server is one arm of the `select!` that also runs the proxy, the browser listener and the web UI, so when `serve_stdio` returns, the entire `aitm` run ends, the proxy included. Before this change stdin EOF, a read error or a line over `MAX_MESSAGE_BYTES` ended it (the last two with an error); a failed or stalled write did not. Now any writer error does too (a stalled parent, a broken pipe, a failed write), and because `serve_lines` returns that error, the process exits nonzero. This is the same "peer is gone" judgment as for a socket, with a larger consequence. Exempting stdio (call 6) is a small change, not a flip: `serve_lines` reads `server.write_timeout()` itself and `serve_stdio` passes no override, so it needs a parameter on `serve_lines` (or a stdio-specific timeout) and a test.
- **Slow-but-live trade-off:** the deadline detects a peer that stops reading, not one that reads slowly. A reader that frees room for each frame within 30 s is never cut, even if it is a trickle; that is indistinguishable from a slow link, and such a peer pins only its own task and a bounded sink, no worse than before this change. A reader that stalls for more than 30 s with full buffers is cut: a browser tab frozen or paused in a debugger. That is acceptable, since the web client reconnects with back-off (`apps/web/src/bus/ws.ts`) and the `lagged` contract already tells it to resync. Replies in flight at expiry are lost, as on any disconnect.

**D4. How the real-socket tests make a never-reading client fill the buffers.** A "stopped" client is a client that never polls its socket after setup. To make the kernel buffers small, the client socket is built with `TcpSocket::new_v4()`, `set_recv_buffer_size(4096)` and `connect`, then upgraded with `tokio_tungstenite::client_async(request, MaybeTlsStream::Plain(stream))`, which yields the same `Ws` type the helpers already use. The server's listener is built with `TcpSocket`, `set_send_buffer_size(4096)`, `bind`, `listen`; accepted sockets inherit it. Both are belt and braces: even if a platform ignores or clamps them, loopback autotuning holds well under 10 MiB while the tests push 16 MiB (the existing AC5 test relies on the same margin with 20 MiB). The server side gets `with_write_timeout(1 s)`.
- Drain arm: subscribe, stop reading, push 4096 flow events with a 4 KiB path each (16 MiB). The 4096 pushes equal the 4096-line sink cap, they do not fall below it; nothing is dropped, because an overflow needs the sink to already hold 4096 lines at the moment of a push, so the bridge really attempts every byte.
- Reply arm: a new in-process command `test.blob` returns a 1 MiB string. A reply carries it twice (in `structuredContent` and again in the rendered text; nothing truncates it), so each reply is about 2 MiB, and the stopped operator's one frame of 16 `test.blob` lines draws about 32 MiB of replies, well inside the 256-line frame cap.
- Assertions never read byte counts from the kernel. Disconnect is observed two ways: the registry (an `Ask`-policy `test.sensitive` probe is refused at once with "no operator is connected", the existing `probe_ask_gate` technique, polled until it flips) and the socket (once the test resumes reading, the stream ends: buffered bytes, then end/reset).
- Non-flakiness comes from margins, not tuning: the registry must flip within `1 s + 5 s` (5x slack on a 1 s timer); the control with the 30 s default is still subscribed after 3 s (10x); the slow reader pauses 10 ms per frame against a 1 s deadline (100x); pushes exceed buffers by at least 16x. Total real time per test is about 2 to 4 s (AC23, which waits out the 5 s refusal budget, takes about 7 s).
- The line transport has fast deterministic twins over `tokio::io::duplex(256)` (a write blocks exactly like a stopped socket) that run in the sandbox, plus one real loopback TCP test. The twins carry the proof of abandonment and cleanup; the TCP test proves only what AC16 says. It rests on one assumption: kernel buffering for the loopback pair stays under the 16 MiB pushed. `LoopbackTcp::bind` offers no way to shrink the server's send buffer, so unlike the bridge tests only the client's receive buffer is small and the margin is autotuning alone.

**D5. Test-support extraction in `aitm` (and why changing the older files is now acceptable).** New `crates/aitm/tests/support/web_bus.rs`, included by each `web_bus_*` test file the way `rig.rs` and `scripted_driver.rs` are (`#[path = "support/web_bus.rs"] #[allow(dead_code)] mod web_bus;`), not through `support/mod.rs`. It holds the union of the three files' helpers: tokens and timeout constants, `temp_ca_dir`, `UnusedClient`, `Parking`/`Release`, the test commands (`test.block`, `test.sleep`, `test.tick`, `test.panic`, `test.sensitive`) and `TestCommands`, `ServerOptions`/`Server`/`start`, `http_flow`/`push_flow`, `Ws` and the connect/send/receive/drain helpers, `open_bus` and friends, `subscribe`, `wait_until`, and the `Ask` probe. #137's AC7 required `web_bus_concurrency` and `web_bus_roles` to pass unchanged so that a green run was independent evidence that the push change, which rewrote the bridge's per-connection state, had not altered PR #103's behaviour: the oracle must not change in the same commit as the thing it checks. That PR has merged, so the oracle did its job, and this is the follow-up AC7 deferred. The oracle is still protected: (a) the extraction commits touch no production file, and the refactored files are run as the oracle against #137's unchanged `bridge.rs`. As shipped, Tasks 4 and 7 ran concurrently, so there was no separate commit before the deadline change; the orchestrator swapped the original `bridge.rs` back in for that run (plan, Task 4 and the amendments); (b) each file keeps a thin file-local wrapper with its old signature (`start`, `open_bus`), so every test body is byte-identical and the diff touches only harness items, imports and the `mod` line; (c) the test names and counts are identical (11 + 5 + 10 = 26), compared with `--list` before and after; (d) optional mutation spot-checks (below). Where the copies differ in strictness the shared helper takes the strictest behaviour that every current test satisfies, or the file keeps its local variant; never the more lenient. One token pair is shared: the tests assert on roles and behaviour, never on the token values.

**D6. Test-support in `aitm-mcp` (Architect's call, deviating from the brief's example path).** There is no `aitm-mcp/tests/support/` today, every copy of the push-server builder is inside `mcp.rs`, and the fixtures it needs (`StubClient`, `ungated`, `http_flow`, `CaptureCmd`, `is_refused`) are used by 60-plus other tests in that file. A new support module would have a single consumer and would force those fixtures to move with it. So the dedupe is one parameterised `push_server_with` builder inside `mcp.rs`'s push section, which `push_server` and the three inline copies use, and every "the reply carries a `sub_id`" assertion in that section goes through `sub_id_of`, or through `assert_subscribed` (a thin wrapper over it) where the id is not used. If Matt prefers a `support/mod.rs`, the same helper moves there mechanically.

**D7. Docs.** Update the `bridge.rs` module doc (the write deadline, and that "a push send fails" is now bounded by it), the `serve_lines` doc, and the doc of `MAX_SUBS_PER_CONN` (point at the total cap). The web-bus-push spec is left as history; this spec supersedes its non-goal "no write timeout".

## Threat/Failure model

| Bound | Defends against | Does not defend against |
|---|---|---|
| `MAX_SUBS_PER_CONN` (32, existing) | One authenticated connection inflating the registry | Many connections |
| `MAX_SUBS_TOTAL` (4096, new) | A token-holder opening many connections to inflate registry size, and with it the lock-hold time of every flow event, state push and audit fan-out; registry memory (< 10 MiB) | See residuals below |
| `HashSet` de-dup | The O(subs x connections) amplification at the cap (CPU under the lock) | Nothing is bounded by it; it changes cost, not delivery |
| Write deadline (30 s) | A peer that stops reading while the server writes: pinned task, FD, subscriptions, a ghost operator keeping `any_role(Operator)` true so agent `Ask` calls park instead of refusing | Residuals below, notably Ping/Pong buffer growth, which is not a send the deadline can time |
| Pre-auth bounds: `HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` (10 s, existing) and the 5 s close-drain budget on the bad-token refusal's reply and Close (the reply's bound is new here) | An unauthenticated peer pinning a task and descriptor, including one that fills the send path with Pongs before sending a bad token. Worst case about 35 s per connection (D3) | Memory growth from Pings and the number of connections (no cap): see residuals below |

Known residuals, accepted:
- **Connection count.** No web listener cap. `MAX_SUBS_TOTAL` is meaningful without one: fan-out cost follows the number of subscriptions, and a connection with none is not in the registry. What stays unbounded is connections themselves (tasks, FDs, each with an idle bounded sink); in practice the process FD limit is the bound. A named follow-up.
- **Starvation.** A holder of the agent token (the lower-privilege one) can fill the 4096 slots with 128 connections of 32 subscriptions each, and keep every one of them reading and discarding what it is sent. Such a peer is live, so the write deadline never cuts it, and the slots stay held until the holder disconnects. A filter that never matches does not help a holder that stops reading: state pushes and the audit line of every audited tool call (`push_state` and `push_agent`, both through `fanout_state`, which ignores filters) are written to every connection holding at least one subscription, so a stopped holder fills its buffers from those and is evicted by the deadline, though slowly on a quiet server. Every later `flow.subscribe`, the operator's included, then fails with the table-full text. With no operator subscribed, `any_role(Operator)` is false, so every `Ask`-gated agent call is refused with "no operator is connected to approve it", and the operator's audit feed goes dark. This fails closed and bypasses nothing: no gated call runs without approval. "The UI still works by polling" holds only because no first-party client calls `flow.subscribe` today; it stops holding when the web UI adopts it (#124). No role reservation in this change (call 9). The recommended mitigation is a slice of the total cap reserved for the Operator role, landed before #124.
- **Ping/Pong write-buffer growth (pre-existing, not addressed here).** The websocket library answers each Ping with an automatic Pong and, when the socket is full, keeps those replies in a user-space buffer with no limit: `bridge.rs` sets only the message and frame sizes, and the library's default `max_write_buffer_size` is `usize::MAX`. A peer that sends Pings and never reads grows server memory about 1:1 with the bytes it sends (the security reviewer measured 3,000,000 pings, 393 MB sent, 508 MB of heap, in 1.4 s). It is reachable before authentication for up to the 10 s handshake window and, with the agent token, after it without limit. The write deadline does not cover it: no send is outstanding for the deadline to time. The follow-up needs its own spec, because replies are uncapped today and the cap is a design call: a write-buffer cap of at least `write_buffer_size` plus the largest legitimate single reply, treating a full buffer as the end of the connection, and/or a ping budget.
- **Per-server caps.** The web and primary servers each have a registry, so the process-wide bound is 2 x 4096; connections with one subscription each still cost O(connections) per audit line (bounded by the FD limit).
- **Trickle readers** that free just enough room per frame are never cut (D3).
- **Idle ghosts.** A half-open peer is written to like any other: state pushes and the audit line of every audited tool call go to every subscribed connection whatever its filter, so its buffers fill and the deadline evicts it. How soon depends on traffic: on a quiet server each line is small, and an idle subscribed operator can stay registered, pinning `any_role(Operator)`, for a long time. The deadline is write-driven; liveness pings are a separate feature.
- **Pre-auth items found by the panel (pre-existing, not addressed here; named follow-ups).** (1) The first line is parsed as JSON in full before any token check (`token_line_matches` in `bridge.rs`, `classify_token` in `transport.rs`), and it may be up to 16 MiB; the security reviewer measured a 16 MiB `[0,0,0,...]` line peaking at about 256 MiB of extra heap in 70 ms, multiplied by the number of parallel connections. A size cap on the first line before parsing is the likely fix (the token is 64 hex characters). (2) The TCP bus waits for the first line with no deadline (`serve_connection` in `transport.rs`), so a silent loopback connection pins a task and a descriptor; the web bridge has `HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`, the TCP bus does not. (3) `serve_lines` has no drop guard, so an unwind or cancellation of the future itself would skip the teardown; the reviewer found no panic source in `read_loop`, so this is theoretical. These, the Ping/Pong growth above and the web listener connection cap are handed to a follow-up session; this change fixes none of them.
- **Refusal audit.** A refused subscribe is audited and fanned to subscribers once per connection, as for the per-connection refusal today; unchanged.

Failure behaviour at expiry: the connection is dropped without a Close frame, replies in flight are lost (as on any disconnect; the effect of a command that already ran stays), logging is `debug` only, except on stdio, where the error is returned out of `main`: it is printed and the process exits nonzero (D3).

## Acceptance criteria

Test names are the contract; file locations are in the plan. "Terminal" marks tests that bind loopback.

**Registry bound**
1. AC1: `MAX_SUBS_TOTAL` is 4096. With 128 connections holding 32 subscriptions each (4096; all 4096 adds succeed), `Subscriptions::add` for a 129th connection returns `Err(SubscribeRefused::TableFull)` and the registry still holds 4096. Test `the_table_cap_refuses_another_connection`.
2. AC2: With the registry full, a closed sink gets `Err(Closed)`, a connection holding 32 gets `Err(TooMany)`, and a connection holding fewer than 32 gets `Err(TableFull)`. Test `refusal_precedence_is_closed_then_per_connection_then_table`.
3. AC3: With the registry full, `remove` of one subscription lets exactly one more `add` succeed and the next gets `TableFull`; `drop_connection` of a connection holding 32 lets 32 adds succeed. Tests `unsubscribe_frees_exactly_one_table_slot`, `ending_a_connection_frees_its_table_slots`.
4. AC4: With the registry full, `flow.subscribe` through `handle_on` returns `isError: true`, content text exactly `flow.subscribe refused: the server's subscription table is full (max 4096 across all connections); try again later`, and no `structuredContent`. Test `a_full_table_refuses_flow_subscribe_with_its_own_text`.
5. AC5: Fan-out still delivers once per connection: a connection with 32 matching subscriptions gets 1 push; a connection whose first subscription does not match and whose second does gets 1 push; `fanout_state` with 4096 connections of one subscription each reaches each exactly once; `fanout_role(Operator, ..)` reaches only operator connections, once each, with 3 subscriptions per connection. Tests `a_connection_with_many_matching_subscriptions_gets_one_push`, `a_non_matching_subscription_does_not_mask_a_matching_one`, `fanout_state_reaches_every_connection_once_at_the_table_cap`, `fanout_role_reaches_only_that_role_once_per_connection`.
6. AC6 (mechanical, not a test): `rg -n "seen: Vec" crates/aitm-mcp/src/server.rs` matches nothing; the three fan-out functions use `HashSet<u64>`.

**Write deadline**
7. AC7: `McpServer::new(..).write_timeout()` equals `Duration::from_secs(30)` and `DEFAULT_WRITE_TIMEOUT` equals it; `.with_write_timeout(Duration::from_secs(7))` reads back 7 s; `.with_write_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` reads back 1 ms. Tests `write_timeout_defaults_to_30_seconds`, `with_write_timeout_sets_it_and_clamps_zero`.
8. AC8: `send_within` returns `Err` (message contains "timed out") within 2 s for a 50 ms limit when the peer never reads a 1 MiB message over a 64 KiB duplex, and returns `Ok` with the full text delivered when the peer reads. 23. AC23 (Terminal): `relay` really uses the bounded refusal. AC22 tests the function over a duplex, so a regression at its call site, `relay` sending the reply with an unbounded `ws.send`, would leave AC22 green. Over real sockets, a raw client with a 4096-byte receive buffer (set before connect) upgrades to `/bus` but never authenticates, against a server whose listener has a 4096-byte send buffer. It sends many 125-byte Pings without reading, so the server's automatic Pongs fill its send path and the client's receive buffer, then sends a bad-token first frame, and still does not read. The refusal send expires at the 5 s close-drain budget and the server drops the connection without writing the reply or a Close. After waiting that budget plus a margin (about 7 s in all), the client resumes reading under a bound and must see (a) the stream end, (b) no text frame containing `unauthorized`, and (c) no Close frame. EOF alone proves nothing: a client that resumes reading sees EOF either way once the connection ends, so the test discriminates on content. Against an unbounded refusal the reply is delivered (and a Close follows it) once the client drains, so the test is red there. It takes about 7 s because the budget is a private 5 s constant (no new config surface). Test `an_unauthenticated_pinging_peer_is_dropped_at_the_refusal_deadline`.

## Open questions

None blocking. Three calls with a security or product flavour are flagged for Matt in the PR body, each with a default and how to flip it:
- Call 9: reserve part of the total cap for the Operator role, so an agent token cannot starve the operator UI of subscriptions. A small change to `Subscriptions::add`, which has no per-role counter to partition, plus a test.
- Call 6: exempt stdio from the deadline, so a stalled stdio parent never ends the stdio connection, which today ends the whole `aitm` run. A small change: `serve_lines` reads the deadline itself and `serve_stdio` passes no override, so it needs a parameter or a stdio-specific timeout, plus a test.
